## 1. What an AOC is

An **Air Operator Certificate (AOC)** is the approval that permits an
organisation to conduct **commercial air transport (CAT)**. It certifies that
the operator has the organisation, management system, personnel, procedures,
and airworthiness arrangements to operate safely. It is issued together with
**operations specifications** that define the permitted scope (aircraft types,
areas of operation, and specific approvals).

An AOC almost never travels alone. A commercial operator typically also needs:

- An **Operating Licence** (economic authorisation — financial and ownership
  fitness) for most commercial operations with relevant aircraft.
- **Continuing airworthiness** arrangements (a **CAMO** approval or contract)
  and **maintenance** (Part-145) arrangements.
- Specific approvals within the ops specs (e.g. PBN, LVO, ETOPS/EDTO,
  dangerous goods, ACMI/wet-lease).

## 2. UK vs EASA after Brexit — the key structural point

Since the UK left the EASA system, there are **two distinct regimes**:

- **United Kingdom:** the **UK Civil Aviation Authority (UK CAA)** is the
  regulator. The applicable rules are the **retained** EU aviation regulations
  as amended in UK law (the UK versions of Reg (EU) 965/2012 on air operations,
  Reg (EU) 748/2012 and Part-CAMO/Part-M airworthiness rules, etc.).
- **European Union / EASA:** an EASA AOC is issued by the **National Aviation
  Authority (NAA)** of an **EU member state** in which the operator has its
  **principal place of business**, under **EASA** rules (Reg (EU) 2018/1139
  and Reg (EU) 965/2012). EASA sets the framework; the member-state NAA
  certifies and oversees. Examples of NAAs: Ireland (IAA), Malta (TM-CAD),
  Austria (Austro Control), Germany (LBA), etc.

**Consequence for the client:** access to both markets can require **two
certificates, two regulators, and two oversight relationships.** Choosing where
to base the EASA operation (which member state) is a genuine strategic decision
— NAA responsiveness, language, cost, tax, and connectivity all matter. Advising
on this is a core Tier 1 service.

## 3. The certification process (five phases)

Both regimes follow a broadly similar, phased certification model. The UK CAA
and most EASA NAAs describe it in five phases:

1. **Pre-application** — initial enquiry, eligibility, and an application
   statement of intent. The authority assesses whether the applicant is a
   credible, appropriately resourced organisation.
2. **Formal application** — the applicant submits the formal application and
   supporting schedule, including the proposed scope, key personnel, and a
   **compliance/gap plan**.
3. **Document evaluation** — the authority reviews the full documentation set:
   management system, manuals, and compliance demonstration. Iterative
   findings and responses are normal here.
4. **Demonstration & inspection** — on-site inspections, interviews with
   nominated/responsible persons, and **demonstration/proving activity**
   (including proving flights where required) to show the procedures work.
5. **Certification** — the AOC and operations specifications are issued, with
   the agreed scope and any limitations.

Ongoing **oversight** (audits, inspections, and continued surveillance) then
runs for the life of the certificate.

## 4. Management system, SMS, and responsible persons

Under the air-operations rules (Part-ORO), the operator must have a
**management system** proportionate to its size and complexity, including:

- An **Accountable Manager** with the authority and funding responsibility for
  the organisation's compliance.
- **Nominated / responsible persons** covering the key functional areas —
  typically **flight operations, crew training, ground operations, and
  continuing airworthiness** — subject to authority acceptance of their
  competence and experience.
- A **Compliance Monitoring** function (independent oversight of compliance).
- A **Safety Management System (SMS)** — safety policy, risk management, safety
  assurance, and promotion.

Getting the right people **accepted** by the authority is one of the most
common critical-path items — hence our postholder-support and interim-provision
services.

## 5. The manual suite

A typical CAT operator documentation set includes:

- **Operations Manual (OM)**:
  - **Part A** — General/Basic (policies and procedures common to all ops)
  - **Part B** — Aircraft Operating Matters (type-specific)
  - **Part C** — Route/Area & aerodrome instructions
  - **Part D** — Training
- **Management System / Compliance Monitoring** documentation and **SMS**
  manual.
- **Minimum Equipment List (MEL)**, developed from the manufacturer's **MMEL**.
- **Continuing Airworthiness Management Exposition (CAME)** and the maintenance
  arrangements (Part-145 / maintenance contract).
- **Training and checking** documentation.

Authoring and maintaining these to authority standard — and keeping them
consistent with each other — is the bulk of the certification workload, and the
reason a **template-driven document factory** (Section 4) is our core
efficiency lever.

## 6. Economic authorisation — the Operating Licence

Separate from the safety AOC, most commercial operators need an **Operating
Licence** demonstrating:

- **Ownership and control** requirements (nationality/control tests differ
  between the UK and the EU).
- **Financial fitness** — the business can meet its obligations for a defined
  period without relying on revenue that has not yet materialised.
- Adequate **insurance**.

The AOC and Operating Licence are typically progressed **in parallel**, and the
ownership/control tests can shape the corporate structure — so early
coordination with the client's corporate lawyers and accountants is essential.

## 7. What this means for scoping an engagement

Every certification engagement is scoped along five axes:

1. **Jurisdiction(s)** — UK, an EASA state, or both.
2. **Operation type** — passenger CAT, cargo, ACMI, business jet charter,
   helicopter/special ops.
3. **Fleet** — number of types (each type multiplies OM Part B, training, and
   airworthiness work).
4. **Special approvals** — PBN, LVO, ETOPS/EDTO, RVSM, dangerous goods,
   HEMS/HHO/offshore, etc.
5. **Client maturity** — greenfield start-up vs an operator with existing
   systems.
