# 01 — Market Analysis

## Market definition

We serve organisations that need to **obtain or maintain** a UK CAA or EASA
AOC and the approvals that surround it. The addressable spend is the external
consulting/advisory budget of these operators — not their total start-up cost.

## Customer segments

| Segment | Typical need | Buying behaviour |
|---------|--------------|------------------|
| **Start-up airlines & CAT operators** | Full AOC from scratch | High urgency, investor-funded, delay-sensitive |
| **Business / executive jet operators** | AOC for charter (Part-CAT / Part-NCC transition) | Owner-operator or management company driven |
| **Cargo & ACMI operators** | AOC + wet-lease approvals | Fleet- and route-driven, fast scaling |
| **Existing operators expanding scope** | New aircraft type, new ops (e.g. LVO, PBN, ETOPS/EDTO, offshore, HEMS) | Incremental, relationship-based |
| **Dual-market operators (post-Brexit)** | A **second** AOC (UK operator wanting EASA access, or vice versa) | Growing niche, high value |
| **Helicopter / special-ops** | AOC + specialist approvals (HEMS, HHO, offshore) | Specialist, higher margin |
| **Investors / lessors / lenders** | Independent due diligence on an operator's certification plan | One-off, advisory, credibility-led |

## Demand drivers

- **UK–EU regulatory divergence** since Brexit: the UK CAA and EASA are now
  distinct regulators. An operator that once held a single EASA AOC covering
  the UK now may need two certificates for full market access.
- **Fleet renewal and new entrants** in business aviation, cargo/e-commerce
  logistics, and regional connectivity.
- **Scope expansion** as operators add types, approvals, and routes — each a
  discrete certification mini-project.
- **Scarcity of in-house expertise.** Nominated/responsible persons and
  authority-facing managers are hard to recruit; interim and fractional
  provision is in demand.

## Competitive landscape

1. **Large / international aviation consultancies** — full-service, credible,
   but expensive and less nimble; can be slow and generalist.
2. **Independent freelancers / ex-authority individuals** — deep expertise,
   low overhead, but limited capacity, key-person risk, and thin on project
   management and document production.
3. **Law firms with aviation practices** — strong on economic licensing and
   corporate structuring, weaker on operational manuals and day-to-day
   authority liaison.
4. **In-house build** — the operator hires postholders directly; slower, and
   they still often need surge support and templates.

### Our positioning

> **Senior expertise delivered as a managed, fixed-scope programme** — the
> reliability and project discipline of a large firm at the responsiveness and
> price of a specialist boutique.

Differentiators:

- **Dual-jurisdiction fluency** (UK *and* EASA) as a core competence, not an
  afterthought.
- **Productised, milestone-priced engagements** — clients know the scope, the
  timeline, and the cost up front.
- **Template-driven document factory** — reusable, authority-tested manual
  frameworks that cut drafting time.
- **Associate network** — surge capacity and specialist approvals without
  carrying full-time overhead.

## Market risks

- Certification volume is **lumpy** and tied to the economic cycle and
  aviation-finance availability.
- Regulatory change can **compress or expand** the value of certain services.
- **Reputation is the whole business** — one botched programme is very costly.
