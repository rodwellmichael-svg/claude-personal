## What this tells us

1. **The household is on track for "Comfortable."** ~£59,180/year (~£4,932/month) lands
   right at the PLSA "Comfortable" couple standard — and that's *before* Michael's NEST pot,
   and on the conservative contribution reading (£32,490/yr). At £49,590/yr it's higher again.
2. **Michael's pension is the engine.** His ~£222k pot plus ~£32,490/year of contributions
   for 8 years grows to ~£634,000 by 65, giving ~£25,400/year of drawdown — the largest
   single income stream in retirement.
3. **A strong guaranteed base underneath it.** Two State Pensions (~£24,500) + Janey's
   Teachers' Pension (~£7,000) = ~£31,500/year secure and inflation-linked. So even if
   investments disappoint, the household stays comfortably above "Minimum" and near "Moderate".
4. **Watch the Annual Allowance.** Michael's ~£32,490/year is within the £60,000 allowance,
   and the taper doesn't bite (threshold income ~£171k, under £200k). But if salary, bonuses
   or contributions rise, keep an eye on both — carry-forward of unused allowance can help.
5. **Tax in retirement.** At ~£38k income Michael would pay basic/higher-rate tax on his
   drawdown; the PLSA figures are indicative rather than after-tax. Sequencing withdrawals
   (using tax-free cash and both people's allowances) can improve the net result.
6. **The £500k mortgage-free home is a backstop** — upside for "Comfortable-plus", care
   costs, or leaving a legacy, not needed for the core plan.
