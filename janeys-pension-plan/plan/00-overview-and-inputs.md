# 00 — Overview & Inputs

## The goal

Give Janey a clear picture of her retirement — **how much she's on track for, how
much she'll need, and what to change** — and a model she can revisit as things move.

The plan answers four questions:

1. **Where is she now?** Pots, contributions, State Pension entitlement.
2. **What does "enough" look like?** A target retirement income and age.
3. **Is there a gap?** Projected pot and income vs. the target.
4. **What closes it?** Contributions, timing, investment approach, tax reliefs.

### Handy benchmark: the "enough" number

The Retirement Living Standards ([PLSA](https://www.retirementlivingstandards.org.uk/))
give a useful target for a **single person** (per year, outside London, illustrative):

| Standard | Rough annual income | What it covers |
|----------|--------------------|----------------|
| **Minimum** | ~£13,400 | Basics, no car, occasional treats |
| **Moderate** | ~£31,700 | More flexibility, a car, one holiday a year |
| **Comfortable** | ~£43,900 | Financial freedom, some luxuries, regular holidays |

*Figures are illustrative and updated periodically — verify the current numbers on
the PLSA site. Couples' figures differ.*

## Assumptions the model will use (defaults, all adjustable)

- **Investment growth:** 5% / year nominal (mid-risk) — scenarios at 3% and 7%.
- **Inflation:** 2.5% / year (results also shown in today's money).
- **Charges:** 0.5% / year platform + fund (adjust to Janey's actual charges).
- **Sustainable withdrawal:** ~4% of the pot per year as a starting rule of thumb,
  or the cost of an annuity — both shown.
- **State Pension:** full new State Pension, added from State Pension age.

> These defaults are placeholders. Once Janey's figures are in, the spreadsheet
> recalculates everything and the scenarios update automatically.
